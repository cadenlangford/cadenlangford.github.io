About Me

Practical problem solving shaped by experience

Growing up on a farm taught me responsibility, persistence, and hands-on problem solving. Maintaining equipment and working through unexpected challenges gave me a habit of staying calm, learning quickly, and looking for better ways to get the job done.

I carried that same mindset into a vending machine business I started in high school. Running the business involved analyzing sales, managing inventory, choosing profitable locations, planning routes, maintaining equipment, and continuously improving daily operations.

That experience introduced me to many of the ideas that later drew me toward industrial engineering. I became interested in understanding how systems work, where inefficiencies occur, and how data can be used to improve performance.

At the University of Tennessee, I have continued building on those experiences through industrial engineering coursework, leadership, technical training, and applied manufacturing work.

Featured Project

Manufacturing Routing & Labor Standardization

I helped reorganize ERP routing structures across chair and cabinet manufacturing, validate labor standards, and improve the accuracy and maintainability of production data. The work supported more reliable labor costing, operational reporting, and future routing maintenance.

My work included conducting time studies, analyzing labor variance reports, restructuring routing logic, reviewing thousands of production records, and building reporting tools for management.

  • Standardized labor routing across chair and cabinet operations.
  • Reorganized routing logic to better match actual production flow.
  • Updated thousands of ERP routing and part records.
  • Improved labor-standard accuracy through time studies and data validation.
  • Built Excel and Power BI tools to support operational decision-making.
